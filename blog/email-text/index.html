The Text Message That Brings Back Customers You Thought Were Gone

Somewhere in your booking system or receipts is a goldmine: people who already paid you once and then drifted away. One honest, well-timed text can bring them back this week. Here's how to send it.

Acquiring a new customer costs real money — ads, discounts, time. Reactivating an old one costs a text message. Yet most small businesses never send it, because they don't know what to say, or they worry it will feel pushy. It won't — if you write it like a person, not a promotion.

This is the reactivation playbook: who to text, when, and the exact words.

Who counts as "gone"?

Start with customers who haven't bought in roughly twice your normal gap. If your typical client returns every 6 weeks, your list is everyone past 12 weeks. If jobs are seasonal, your list is last season's customers. You don't need fancy software — a spreadsheet export from your booking or payment system, sorted by last visit, is enough. Start with your 50 most recent lapsed customers; they're the warmest.

The message (steal this)

"Hi [Name], it's [Your name] from [Business]. We realized it's been a while and wanted to check in — hope you're doing well! If you need [service] again, we'd love to take care of you. Just reply here and we'll get you booked. — [Business]"

Why this works, line by line: it's from a named human, not a brand blast. It acknowledges the gap without guilt-tripping. It asks for nothing except a reply. And it opens a conversation — the replies, even the "not yet, thanks," are future business and free market research.

The optional nudge: if you want an offer, keep it simple and time-bound — "This month, returning customers get [small perk]." A reason to act now, not a desperate discount.

Timing and follow-up rules

  • Send Tuesday–Thursday, late morning. Texts sent during business hours get business-hour replies. Avoid early mornings, late nights, and Mondays.
  • One follow-up, max. No reply in 5–7 days? One short nudge ("Just floating this back up — happy to help whenever you're ready"). Then stop. Silence is an answer.
  • Reply fast when they do. A reactivation conversation that waits four hours for your answer often dies. The whole point is a conversation.
  • Always honor "stop." Immediately, no questions. It's the law for marketing texts and it's also just decent.

What to expect

Realistically, a well-built lapsed list answers at rates businesses find shocking — these are people who already know and liked you; many simply forgot, moved their routine, or didn't realize they were due. Even a handful of returning customers from a 50-person list pays for the effort many times over. And the ones who reply "we moved away" or "switched to X" have handed you honest feedback you'd never otherwise hear.

Make it a habit, not an event: 10 lapsed customers, every Monday, five minutes. That's 500 personal touches a year — a pipeline no ad budget can match at this price.
