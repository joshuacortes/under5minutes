How to Write an FAQ Page That AI Assistants Love to Quote

When someone asks an AI assistant a question about businesses like yours, the answer often comes from a page written exactly like this one. Here's how to make that page yours.

SEO & AI Search · 6 min read · By the Under 5 Minutes team

AI assistants answer questions by finding pages that already contain the answer, clearly stated. FAQ pages are the perfect shape for that: a real question, followed by a direct answer. Businesses with good FAQ pages don't just rank better — they get quoted. Here's how to write one that works for both humans and machines.

What questions should your FAQ page answer?

Start with the questions customers actually ask you — the ones you answer by phone every week:

  • Do you offer [specific service]? How much does it typically cost?
  • Do I need an appointment, or do you take walk-ins?
  • What areas do you serve?
  • How long does [service] take?
  • Do you offer estimates / guarantees / financing?
  • What should I do before my appointment?

Eight to twelve real questions beat thirty invented ones. If a question has never been asked by a human, cut it.

How should each answer be written?

Follow the format AI systems (and skimming humans) prefer:

  • Answer first. The first sentence answers the question completely: "Yes, we offer same-day appointments for most repairs."
  • Then add one or two sentences of detail. Specifics — prices ranges, timeframes, areas by name — make your answer the most useful one available.
  • Use the words customers use. If people say "AC fix," don't write only "HVAC repair services." Match real language.
  • Keep it honest and current. An FAQ that overpromises trains customers — and AI — to distrust you.

Where should the FAQ live?

Put your top 5 questions on your homepage or main service page (that's where AI and Google look first), and the full set on a dedicated FAQ page linked in your footer. Then mirror the most important ones onto your Google Business Profile's Q&A section — you can post and answer your own questions there, as covered in our Maps tune-up guide.

Write new answers in five-minute sittings. Every time a customer asks you something new, that's your next FAQ entry — draft it on the spot and add it that week.

Quick answers

Does an FAQ page help regular SEO too? Yes — FAQs match the long, question-shaped searches people type and speak, and they can earn rich results in Google.

How long should answers be? Two to four sentences for most. Long enough to be complete, short enough to be quoted whole.
