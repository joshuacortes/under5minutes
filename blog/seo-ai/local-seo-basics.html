Local SEO Basics: The 5 Signals That Actually Matter (and the 50 That Don't)

You could spend a year on SEO tips and miss the handful that move the needle for a local business. Here they are, in order — no jargon, no fluff.

SEO & AI Search · 6 min read · By the Under 5 Minutes team

Search "local SEO tips" and you'll drown: schema, citations, keyword density, domain authority. Most of it matters at the margins or not at all for a business serving one town. What follows is the short list — the five signals that consistently decide whether a local business gets found — based on what Google itself documents and what practitioners measure year after year.

Signal 1: Your Google Business Profile

For local searches, your profile is your SEO. Category accuracy, services, hours, photos, and attributes drive the map pack, which is where local clicks live. Nothing else on this list outperforms a complete, active profile. (Full walkthrough: our Maps tune-up checklist.)

Signal 2: Reviews — count, rating, recency

Reviews influence both ranking and choice. A steady flow of recent reviews at 4.3 stars or better is the strongest trust signal a local business can build, and it's free. If you only build one habit from this article, build the review habit.

Signal 3: A homepage that says what and where

Your homepage should state, in its headline and first paragraph, your service and your city in plain words. Businesses hide this behind slogans ("Quality you can trust since 1998") and then wonder why Google can't tell who to show them to. Clarity is a ranking factor you control completely.

Signal 4: Consistent name, address, phone everywhere

Every listing — Google, Yelp, Apple Maps, Facebook, directories — should show identical details. Inconsistencies (old address, different phone, "St." vs "Street" is fine, but a different number is not) dilute Google's confidence in your data, and confidence is what's being ranked.

Signal 5: Pages for each service and area

One page per core service, written for the humans in each town you serve, gives Google something precise to match against precise searches. This is the only "content" item on the list — and it matters after the first four are handled, not before.

What about everything else?

Keywords in your business name (don't — it's against guidelines and risks suspension), buying links, blogging daily "for SEO," hiring someone to "submit you to 500 directories" — marginal at best, harmful at worst. The five signals above are the job. Everything else is decoration.

Quick answers

How long does local SEO take to work? Profile fixes can show within days; meaningful ranking movement typically builds over 4–12 weeks of consistent signals.

Do I need to blog for local SEO? Not for its own sake. A few strong service pages and a healthy profile outperform a neglected blog every time.
